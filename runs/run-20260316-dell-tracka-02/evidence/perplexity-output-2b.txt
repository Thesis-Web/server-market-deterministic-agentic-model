Paywalled research almost certainly exists, but open summaries are sparse. [INFERENCE — not sourced]

Telecom standards bodies (edge/MEC/Open RAN hardware requirements)
ETSI GS MEC 003 (“Multi-access Edge Computing (MEC); Framework and Reference Architecture”) describes a framework for MEC hosts and orchestration, including the information a MEC orchestrator uses when selecting a MEC host: required virtualized resources (compute, storage, network, specific hardware support), required MEC features, required connectivity, and deployment considerations. While it is not a server spec, it indirectly drives hardware requirements (e.g., servers must expose needed accelerators and meet virtualization requirements). Source: ETSI GS MEC 003 v2.0.1, 2025-06-27 (framework document; scanned via Scribd mirror), https://www.scribd.com/document/557849926/gs-mec003v020101p.
​

Within ETSI MEC 003, the host selection criteria emphasize scalable compute, storage, and network resources, and some explicit “specific hardware support” for MEC applications, implying requirements like accelerators, sufficient NICs, and high I/O throughput on edge servers to support MEC workloads. Source: same ETSI GS MEC 003 v2.0.1.

[INFERENCE — I did not find, in open sources, a GSMA, TM Forum, or O‑RAN Alliance document that explicitly defines server hardware SKUs or vendor selection criteria for edge servers; available specs focus on functional and interface requirements rather than named server vendors.]

I4. Dell PowerEdge power efficiency — published data (2024–2026)

A Prowess Consulting study, commissioned by Dell, reports that upgrading to the latest Dell PowerEdge servers with AMD EPYC CPUs can reduce CPU power consumption per operation by up to 65% versus previous‑generation Dell servers, enable up to a 7:1 consolidation ratio, and cut per‑core VMware licensing costs by more than 42%. The study also claims up to 47% higher performance than “competing solutions,” linking efficiency and performance. Source: “Dell™ PowerEdge™ Servers Deliver Higher Efficiency and Performance, New Prowess Consulting Study Shows” – Prowess Consulting press release, 2025-05-21, https://prowessconsulting.com/press-release/dell-poweredge-servers-deliver-higher-efficiency-and-performance/.

The Prowess release emphasizes TCO factors such as reduced energy expenses and lower licensing costs as a result of consolidation and better per‑watt performance, but it does not publish vendor‑by‑vendor energy consumption comparisons in detail; instead it summarizes the benefits for organizations upgrading to new PowerEdge vs prior Dell platforms and unnamed “competing solutions.” Source: Prowess Consulting, 2025-05-21.
